# ASC3ND Next-Wave Operating Document

## Purpose

This document captures only the nonprofit operating ideas from the StarNet source-intelligence archive that are directly relevant to THE ASC3ND COLLECTIVE. It deliberately excludes the unrelated credit/business-funding material from the Monica Main channel.

Bring this workstream forward after the current website proof build is complete: founders, Community Cuts proof system, project structure, media workflow, footer polish, and first project population.

The source archive repeatedly supports a few practical ideas for small nonprofits: funders inspect online credibility before engaging; fundraising works better as a repeatable system than as one-off asks; donor relationships matter more than urgency messaging; the core fundraising materials can be standardized; AI can reduce research and drafting time when organizational facts and quality checks are controlled; sponsorships work better as partnerships; board roles need to be explicit; and claims about funding outcomes must be verified before use.

## PART 1 — WORK THE AI AGENT CAN HANDLE

### Public trust and discoverability

1. Run a recurring public trust audit for ASC3ND: website, Google results, social profiles, Candid/GuideStar, public nonprofit records, donation links, and consistency of organization name/contact information.
2. Keep the website current, clear, and evidence-led. Maintain the Trust & Transparency page and flag inconsistencies or stale information.
3. Maintain a canonical organization facts file so every grant, donor, sponsor, directory, and website workflow uses the same approved facts.
4. Prepare and maintain directory/profile content for Candid and other approved nonprofit discovery systems once eligibility and credentials are available.

### Fundraising operating system

5. Build and maintain five core fundraising assets: case for support, donor list, appeal template, thank-you template, and sponsorship deck.
6. Maintain donor/prospect segmentation based on relationship, repeat engagement, capacity signals, interests, event participation, volunteer activity, and prior giving history where lawfully available.
7. Generate relationship-building touch plans before major asks. Draft personalized update, thank-you, invitation, follow-up, and stewardship communications for human approval.
8. Build a monthly donor workflow: candidate identification, invitation copy, onboarding, acknowledgment, retention touches, and reporting.
9. Build corporate-partnership research briefs that focus on mission fit, community overlap, employee engagement, in-kind value, sponsorship, and long-term partnership rather than cold pitching alone.
10. Prepare sponsorship packages from approved facts, event proof, audience, deliverables, partner value, and real opportunities. Never invent reach, attendance, or benefits.

### Grants and institutional funding

11. Maintain a reusable grant knowledge base with organization facts, mission, leadership, programs, project descriptions, budgets, outcomes, prior language, attachments, and approved evidence.
12. Research grant opportunities and score them for mission fit, geography, eligibility, timing, reporting burden, likely competitiveness, and documentation requirements.
13. Draft LOIs, grant narratives, work plans, budgets, attachment checklists, and submission packets from approved organizational facts.
14. Run proposal quality checks for consistency, compliance, unsupported claims, missing fields, tone, duplicated language, and funder-specific requirements.
15. Maintain a grant calendar and follow-up queue for deadlines, reports, renewals, and relationship touches.
16. Never reuse an AI-generated proposal without a quality-control pass and never insert facts, metrics, partnerships, awards, tax status, or outcomes that are not verified.

### Donor communications and campaigns

17. Audit fundraising language for fear, desperation, vague claims, and organization-centered framing. Favor calm, consistent, donor-relevant communication tied to real possibilities and real proof.
18. Maintain an editorial calendar for donor updates, project proof, founder stories, volunteer stories, partner recognition, and giving opportunities.
19. Draft fundraising emails, landing-page copy, donation-page copy, direct-mail drafts, social fundraising copy, and thank-you sequences from approved facts.
20. Test clarity of asks: who is being asked, what for, why now, what happens next, and how the donor will know the work occurred.
21. Maintain a campaign checklist so every campaign has a goal, audience, case, proof, ask, follow-up, thank-you, reporting, and post-campaign review.

### Board and governance support

22. Create board-role orientation materials, meeting packets, dashboards, fundraising expectations, committee charters, and action trackers from policies approved by the organization.
23. Prepare board-facing summaries of funding pipeline, donor activity, project progress, risks, deadlines, and decisions requiring votes or signatures.
24. Maintain a governance document inventory and flag missing or outdated policies without fabricating them.

### Measurement and learning

25. Build a project evidence record for every ASC3ND project: approved photos/video, dates, partners, activities, outputs, outcomes, quotes, consent status, and supporting documents.
26. Turn completed project evidence into funder-ready project summaries, donor updates, sponsorship proof, grant attachments, annual-report material, and website content.
27. Maintain a source/evidence link for every public quantitative claim. Claims that cannot be substantiated remain unpublished.
28. Produce a quarterly fundraising and visibility review: what was attempted, what converted, what stalled, what needs human attention, and what should change next.

## PART 2 — WORK A HUMAN MUST DO

### Legal authority and compliance

1. Sign government, tax, banking, grant, charity-registration, or platform certifications that legally require an officer, director, authorized representative, or person signing under penalty of perjury.
2. Provide or approve the organization EIN, IRS determination/status documents, Washington charitable-registration information, banking details, government identification, KYC information, and other restricted credentials when legitimately required.
3. Approve legal representations about tax deductibility, charitable status, financial condition, governance, conflicts, insurance, youth safety, privacy, and regulated activity.
4. Approve contracts, sponsorship agreements, grant agreements, fiscal commitments, vendor agreements, and material policy changes.

### Organizational truth and strategic decisions

5. Confirm the mission, priorities, programs, populations served, geography, operating model, budget, goals, and what ASC3ND will or will not promise.
6. Decide which programs or projects move forward and which fundraising opportunities are appropriate for the organization.
7. Approve budgets, fundraising goals, sponsorship pricing/value exchanges, grant budgets, restricted-fund commitments, and spending decisions.
8. Confirm board membership, officer roles, governance actions, and any decisions requiring board authorization.

### Relationships and asks

9. Conduct or participate in high-trust donor, major-gift, funder, sponsor, government, school, family, youth, and community-partner conversations where authentic human relationship is material.
10. Make or explicitly authorize major financial asks and relationship-sensitive solicitations. The AI agent can prepare research, scripts, briefs, and follow-up, but should not impersonate a founder or human relationship owner.
11. Approve donor recognition, partner recognition, testimonials, public quotes, and sensitive stewardship decisions.

### Evidence, consent, and media

12. Supply or authorize real project facts, photos, video, participant quotes, attendance numbers, outcomes, budgets, and partner names.
13. Confirm consent/release status for youth, families, volunteers, staff, and other identifiable people before public media use.
14. Select final hero imagery, sensitive testimonials, and any material that could affect participant privacy, dignity, safety, or reputation.
15. Verify the truth of project outcomes and impact statements before publication or fundraising use.

## Operating rules

AI should proceed autonomously on research, drafting, organization, consistency checking, website/profile maintenance, pipeline management, and evidence preparation. Human review is reserved for legal authority, protected credentials, binding commitments, strategic truth, high-trust relationships, consent, and final factual approval.

Do not use numerical fundraising promises from the source archive as ASC3ND claims. The archive itself flags many fundraising amounts, timelines, matching-gift statistics, platform claims, and AI-efficiency claims as requiring current verification.
